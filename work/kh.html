About the project

I came to K&H to see the corporate side: a big design team, where I wouldn’t have to solve everything alone. That only partly came true. I still ran several projects at once, and many of them were complex enough on their own.

My main work was the website: building its design system, and with it, a way of working with the developers.

Before the first component

There was no documentation for the CMS, not even for the developers. It took months of research and meetings with them just to understand how the site works, and that is how a working relationship with the developers began.

Until then, designers at K&H worked on their own, mostly for the app. That worked there, but the website needed a completely different structure.

A system from a single page

There was no time to build the system from the ground up. It had to be worked out from one subpage, and we knew it wouldn’t hold. At the start you can’t know what a component will have to carry, or in how many combinations.

The developers and I saw the trouble coming, and we argued with the business about it many times. In the end both sides paid for it, design and development alike. We corrected what we could.

A system like this needs documentation, annotations and constant syncing, because the needs change from month to month.

For the forms, I annotated every element for the developers: the matching component in their library, a link to its latest documentation, and every spacing, so what they build matches Figma.

One token, every platform

My biggest pain was that the app and web systems were never fully separated. The design system was tokenised at the primitive level, and that couldn’t be changed. So any change ran through every platform, even the ones the web developers had no way to adjust.

Keeping the two apart would have been much safer in the long run.

Takeaways

  • No good system without developers. Some systems can’t be finished in Figma. I had to understand what the developers build, think the way they do, and keep the design system in sync with theirs.
  • A marathon, not a sprint. The limits are stricter here, and sometimes hard to understand. You still move forward, only slower, so it’s better to go slowly and surely.
  • Small wins count. Even a copy change goes through rounds of agreement, so every step that ships is worth being glad about.
  • More accepting, not helpless. I learned where to push and where to let go, without giving up on the system.
