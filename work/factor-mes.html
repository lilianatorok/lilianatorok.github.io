About the project

Factor-MES coordinates industrial machines and manages their efficiency. It measures and optimises the performance of large-scale machines such as lathes and drilling machines, hall by hall across the plant. I took the project over with an atomic system and the screens built on it, and kept designing new screens as the needs grew.

My role

I designed from the project manager’s brief, adding ideas for a better experience within what the atomic system allowed, and we reviewed each round together.

My design tasks

  • New screens
  • Fixes from tests
  • Smoother processes
  • Light mode
  • Tablet views

Data at a glance

Every screen is full of numbers about how the machines are doing, and they have to be read in a moment. So the data speaks through graphs, bar charts and progress bars.

Monitoring

Charts that fit

Bar charts and progress bars were used at the wrong sizes, so I corrected and re-placed them, and suggested to the client what else the pages could show.

The man in gloves

The same screens are used at a desk and on the factory floor, where the so-called “man in gloves” works on a tablet with gloves on. Everything had to work for both of them, and the tablet views became the biggest challenge.

For the tablet I combined desktop and mobile elements, whichever was simpler to use. The kanban cards, for example, got the desktop layout in portrait and the mobile one in landscape. After several rounds of consultations and prototypes, the buttons got a size that worked with gloves on.

70 px

The minimum button height on the kanban cards, according to the tests.

What else I worked on

Mobile

A lighter header

I redesigned the mobile header and view changer to be airier and easier to use. The view changer (the second row) can be switched off to save space.

All screens

Light mode, same contrast

I made a light mode for every existing page, and added its colour styles to the atomic system with a contrast that matches the dark mode.

Takeaways

I took the project over from another designer, and some of the earlier design errors only came to light later. Fixing them taught me the most.

What I fixed, and learned

  • Plan the breakpoints first. The screens I took over were designed for four widths only: 375, 768, 1024 and 1920. Filling in the gaps later taught me to set the breakpoints before the first screen.
  • A system is only as good as its smallest parts. Auto-layout was used the wrong way and components were missing states, and every screen was built from them. Fixing them from the bottom up fixed the screens too.
  • Data needs density. Huge padding made data-heavy cards too big, and the header and sidebar took space from them. I tightened them, so the data got the room.
  • Readability starts with the font. Small text was hard to read, so I chose a rounder, more readable font, and raised the contrast with darker backgrounds and brighter text.
