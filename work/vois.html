About the project

I came to VOIS to work in an international team, and to see what it’s like to be part of an even bigger organisation.

Vodafone acquired Three, and Three is the brand I work on. It has to become part of Vodafone while keeping what makes it Three. Even the design system changes: Three moves onto Vodafone’s, reskinned with its own brand. That’s the main project: helping the brand through the change, step by step. Meanwhile, we keep everything else running with a long list of everyday projects.

I can’t show the work from here yet. What I can show is how it goes, and what I’ve learned along the way.

Handover, week two

In my second week I flew to the UK for a handover with the Vodafone side. I came with a long list of questions. They hadn’t prepared much either, so every answer took a few more questions to get out, and some answers only turned into new questions.

Still, I came home with what the team needed most: a stakeholder map, how the squads run, and the workflows behind them.

The squad

Back home I joined a squad with colleagues from the UK, India and Egypt. Here a lot of people have to line up with a lot of things at once, so agreeing on anything takes longer. Sometimes an answer takes a day, or more.

It’s also the first place where I’m this far from the developers, and that is very new for me.

The pace is what you’d expect from a big corporation. Projects move slowly, through a lot of stakeholders.

A new generation

Besides the squad work, I’m on a project that isn’t tied to any one squad. The brand wants to reach a new generation, so its features have to change with it, across the whole website and app, from the sales pages to any journey that follows.

It’s brainstorming with the stakeholders. I bring ideas from outside the box, and together we narrow them down until they’re feasible and fit into how we build. Some are small, some are long term.

Takeaways so far

  • Shared knowledge is the biggest knowledge. Here, information comes and goes, often without being written down or shared, and people work on features without knowing about each other. Across these distances, documentation is one of the greatest treasures: open to everyone, and clear to everyone.
  • Every culture talks differently. People from other countries communicate in other ways. Building relationships matters just as much as anywhere, but with this many people and this much distance, it’s a lot harder.
  • Know where your role ends. Unlike at a small agency, I can’t take part in every step. Who owns what is strictly set. We don’t write specifications at all, a separate team does. Still, they’re worth reading: that’s how you get to know the system you design for.
